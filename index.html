Selected work

Seven problems from the HighSpark account: what I did about each one, the number that moved, and what I'd say differently now.

Seasonal launch

Rakhi 2026 sold 3.6x the previous season

₹6.28L → ₹22.33LRakhi sales, July + August, 2025 vs 2026
875 → 2,518 units 41% from 8 new designs 2.1x on the 25 returning designs

Eight new designs brought ₹9.09L. The 25 designs carried over from 2025 sold ₹13.24L, twice what they did the year before. Cross-checked against the Business Report at ₹22.29L.

Not yet provenProfit. Ads ran at 31.5% of Rakhi sales through 21 Aug, the last selling days have no ad export, and there's no product-cost data. This is sales growth, not efficiency, and I present it that way.

Advertising economics

61% of ad-attributed sales wasn't the product being advertised

25.1% → 64.3%Blended ACOS vs direct ACOS
Situation

A ₹29.1L / 90-day Sponsored Products budget, judged on a healthy-looking blended ACOS.

Approach

Split Amazon's ad "sales" into the advertised SKU's own sales and halo, then recomputed ACOS per SKU.

Result

Direct ACOS was 2.5x worse. At a 30% bar, 146 SKUs carrying ₹5.06L/month flip from winner to loser.

The counter-rule30 SKUs look bad on direct ACOS but quietly pay for sales elsewhere. Cutting on direct ACOS alone would have killed them.

Judgement

The ₹6.5L "wasted spend" headline I chose not to present

46,000 → 639Zero-order matches vs pairs worth a decision

A first pass at zero-order ad spend came out at roughly ₹6.5L a month. Most of it was ~46,000 auto-targeting long-tail matches at ₹1–50 each: noise that auto campaigns are meant to produce. I filtered down to 639 search-term / campaign pairs with enough spend to act on.

What I learned sinceZero orders in a window isn't proof of waste. A 187-term negative list built this way went on to take 105 orders at 40.9% ACOS in October, and about 40% of such a list changes status within 16 days.

Returns & customer experience

Returns weren't one quality problem. They were two problems with two owners.

22.1%of gross sales returned, ₹13.6L in 30 days
51% sizing & fit 31% undeliverable or refused ~6% genuine quality

I categorised all 1,489 return reasons instead of debating the headline rate. Sizing is a listing-content job (size charts, how-to-measure images). Refusals are an ops, courier and COD job. One blurry concern became two workstreams, each with its own owner in the weekly review.

The detail that set priorityThe worst-returning SKU (₹52.7k returned) was also one of the best ad performers. Fixing its sizing improves a winner instead of rescuing a lost cause.

Budget allocation

The most efficient category was getting 8% of the budget

21.2% ACOSNose pins and rings, on ₹2.39L of ₹29.1L
53 SKUs, 2% of catalogue 33% star-performer rate vs ~12% Pendants

Meanwhile two pendant SKUs were absorbing a quarter of their own category's budget on targeting already tagged inefficient. I recommended both moves, not either/or: fix the Pendant waste, and put the next incremental rupee into nose pins.

Honest follow-upThe 10–21 Oct test showed no visible category-level lift: share of revenue went 15.9% → 13.4% → 15.7%, in a festive month. Still open.

Data integrity

An 8x error in ad attribution, stopped before it drove a decision

9 defectscaught by my review process before any decision used them
8x sales understated 3x spend double-count 30-day file posing as 90-day

A sensible-looking dedup rule cut attributed sales ~8x and produced a 99% blended ACOS. Every number downstream was internally consistent. Checking it against the Search Term report, reading ~26%, exposed the bad assumption.

The rule it producedInternal consistency is not validation. A new source's headline ratio gets checked against an independent one before anyone trusts it. Three of the nine catches came from that one rule.

Systems

365 MB of raw exports, turned into a question answered in a tenth of a second

38x fasterMeasured: 0.10s vs 3.9s on the 135 MB, 200k-row ads file
Situation

Weekly reporting meant opening a dozen Amazon exports by hand. It took a full day and only answered questions someone thought of in advance.

Approach

A SQL layer (DuckDB / Parquet, 42 views, incremental refresh) that cleans Amazon's columns on load, with a weekly Command Center workbook regenerated on top of it.

Result

2,420 SKUs from 20+ sources in one filterable action sheet. The second and third question now cost nothing to ask.

The brief"I don't have time to read every report." So the deliverable is one sheet that answers "what needs action", not eight dashboards.

How I work

Four habits, each one learned from a number that turned out to be wrong.

01

State the universe

The same 30 days in this account reads ₹65.5L, ₹61.3L or ₹54.8L depending on which population you count. All three are correct. A number without its definition isn't a number.

02

Cross-verify before it lands

Anything going into a decision gets checked two or three independent ways: ads against orders against the Business Report. They agree, or the gap gets explained.

03

Calibrate down, not up

The impressive version of a finding is usually the one that hasn't been filtered yet. I'd rather bring a smaller number that survives questioning.

04

Automate the repeatable part

Manual review stops scaling at about thirty rows. A scripted overlap check caught ~12 copy violations in one 80-SKU batch that human review had already passed.

Built

The tools the account runs on, built with AI agents as a pair-programmer.

⌘ Excel

Command Center

The weekly ops workbook: action sheet, ad performance, restock planner, flag log, category dashboard. Regenerated from raw exports, never hand-edited.

⌘ SQL

Query layer

DuckDB / Parquet over ~365 MB of Amazon CSV. 42 views, incremental refresh, measured 38x faster than the approach it replaced.

⌘ Agents

Five Claude Code skills

Weekly refresh, flat-file uploads and parent/child variations, listing copy, image-upload prep and suppression cases. Each turns a multi-hour task into a reviewed, repeatable run.

⌘ Copy

Listing copy system

Formula-driven titles and five bullets from one catalogue sheet, with five product-type grammar branches across 260 SKUs.

⌘ Guards

Automated quality checks

A SKU-alignment assertion on every write, plus scripted word-overlap and duplicate-phrase checks on generated copy.

⌘ Docs

Operating manual

A written list of every measurement trap found in the account: averaged rate columns, line-total revenue, overlapping report windows, halo. Each one stays fixed for whoever comes next.
